# Bibaling Slovenian Verse Guide — v4

Use this guide as a positive reference for Slovenian children’s-book adaptation.

Learn the principles. Do not mechanically copy the examples.

# TARGET FEEL

Good Bibaling verse should feel:

- natural in the mouth;
- musical without sounding manufactured;
- simple without becoming flat;
- warm, vivid, and playful;
- faithful to the illustration and emotional beat;
- pleasant for an adult to repeat many times.

The goal is not literal translation.

The goal is a Slovenian read-aloud experience that feels intentionally written.

# POETIC WORD ORDER

Do not treat ordinary prose order as the only acceptable Slovenian order.

Poetry may use marked word order when:
- it improves cadence;
- the meaning remains clear;
- the line sounds intentional aloud;
- a native speaker approves it;
- it strengthens a repeated refrain.

Approved example:

Kako zelo imam te rad!

Why it can work:
- the final “te rad” gives the refrain a strong landing;
- the marked order feels poetic rather than accidental;
- it is memorable and emotionally direct;
- the parent explicitly approved its rhythm.

Do not automatically rewrite it as:

Kako zelo te imam rad!

The ordinary version may be more neutral prose, but it is not automatically better verse.

Editorial lesson:

Judge a refrain by the whole book’s voice, cadence, and native-speaker response—not ordinary prose order alone.

# APPROVED REFRAIN STRATEGY 1

Kako zelo imam te rad!

What it preserves:
- explicit declaration of love;
- strong rhythmic ending;
- universal emotional meaning.

What it loses:
- direct mushroom wordplay.

Useful structure:

[first rhyming pair]
[first rhyming pair]
Kako zelo imam te rad!
[line that rhymes naturally with rad]

Do not overuse rad / zaklad / grad / hlad. Change the structure when natural options run out.

# APPROVED REFRAIN STRATEGY 2

Ti si moja ljuba goba!

What it preserves:
- mushroom identity;
- affectionate tone;
- a memorable Slovenian book-level device;
- no narrator-gender complication.

What it changes:
- it adapts the emotional meaning rather than translating “I love you so much” literally.

Useful structure:

Ti si moja ljuba goba,
[line that rhymes naturally]
[second rhyming pair]
[second rhyming pair]

Risk:

Do not overuse goba / soba / podoba / roba.

# BOTH REFRAINS CAN BE GOOD

Do not force one “correct” solution.

“Kako zelo imam te rad!” and “Ti si moja ljuba goba!” solve different parts of the source.

One complete version may preserve emotional meaning.

Another may preserve mushroom identity and wordplay.

That is a legitimate reason to give the parent two polished versions.

# STRONG EXAMPLE — NATURAL AABB

Ti si moja ljuba goba,
v temi žari tvoja podoba.
Ko pade noč, zasiješ za nas,
čarobna lučka sveti ves čas.

Why it works:
- goba / podoba and nas / čas work aloud;
- word order is clear;
- imagery matches the scene;
- vocabulary is simple;
- each line contributes meaning.

Do not reuse the same rhyme words repeatedly elsewhere.

# SHORT REFRAIN AS A BUTTON

Moja ljuba goba!

A short refrain can sit after a complete stanza rather than carrying the rhyme itself.

Use this when forcing the refrain into the stanza weakens the verse.

# REFRAIN CASE STUDY: ENGLISH MUSH/MUCH PUN

The English source combines:
- “I love you so much”;
- mushroom imagery;
- “mush” as affectionate wordplay.

A Slovenian version cannot preserve every layer literally.

Valid strategies include:

1. Explicit love:
   Kako zelo imam te rad!

2. Mushroom affection:
   Ti si moja ljuba goba!

3. Short affectionate tag:
   Moja ljuba goba!

4. Two complete versions using different strategies.

Do not call one strategy wrong merely because it preserves a different layer.

# NATIVE-PARENT EDITORIAL RULE

If the parent says:
- “this has better rhythm”;
- “this is perfect”;
- “this does not rhyme”;
- “we would not say it like that”;

treat that as central editorial evidence.

Do not override it with mechanical scansion or ordinary prose preferences.

If the parent approves exact wording, preserve it exactly until asked to revise it.

# CREATIVE SELECTION PROCESS

Before showing one couplet:

1. Draft at least five different sentence structures.
2. Test each aloud internally.
3. Remove filler and accidental inversions.
4. Remove source drift.
5. Remove weak rhyme.
6. Keep poetic word order when it genuinely improves cadence.
7. Choose the strongest surviving option.

# NATURAL DOES NOT MEAN PROSAIC

A line may be:
- grammatically marked;
- compressed;
- reordered;
- more lyrical than everyday speech;

and still be good children’s verse.

Reject marked word order only when:
- it sounds accidental;
- meaning becomes unclear;
- it exists solely to reach a rhyme;
- native-speaker reading feels awkward.

# CHILD-FRIENDLY DOES NOT MEAN DIMINUTIVE

Do not automatically create:
- vodice;
- posodice;
- zgodbice;
- lučkice;
- mamice;

merely because they sound childlike or rhyme.

Use a diminutive only when it is natural, adds warmth or scale, and is not a rhyme crutch.

# BAD — RHYME-FORCED LANGUAGE

Poljubček v lase ji mama da,
po stopnicah navzdol se nato poda.

Why it fails:
- “se nato poda” sounds written for rhyme;
- the adult reader hears the machinery;
- poetic order is not the problem—the weak phrase is.

# BAD — SUGARY DIMINUTIVES

Mala lama hoče malo vodice,
mama pri koritu pomiva posodice.

Why it fails:
- vodice / posodice feels generated;
- diminutives do the rhyming work;
- the language becomes cute rather than good.

# BAD — PROSE BROKEN INTO LINES

Hoče piti.
Mama je v kuhinji pri koritu.

Why it fails:
- understandable but shapeless;
- no memorable cadence, image, or device.

Simple language still needs literary form.

# BAD — INVENTED FILLER

Vodo bi pila! si mala želi,
mama pri koritu posodo drži.

Why it fails:
- “posodo drži” adds no meaningful image;
- it exists mainly to rhyme;
- it weakens the source.

# BAD — WEAK RHYME PAIRS

pravi / zabavi  
bolj / dol  
je / že  
kliče / pokliče

Problems may include:
- unsatisfying sound or stress;
- partial rhyme;
- repeated word stem;
- mechanical selection.

# RHYTHM QUALITY

Do not count syllables and declare success.

Listen for:
- lexical stress;
- strong-beat spacing;
- awkward word boundaries;
- long weak stretches;
- paired lines wanting incompatible reading speeds.

Bad example:

Kosmati prijatelj na drevesu sedi,
z visoke veje name pazi in bedi.

Problem:

“prijatelj na” catches in natural speech. End rhyme does not repair the line.

# FINAL CHECK

Before showing a stanza, ask:

- Is it enjoyable aloud?
- Does it fit the picture?
- Is the emotional beat intact?
- Is the rhyme real?
- Is the vocabulary suitable?
- Is any unusual word order purposeful?
- Did I choose this because it is good, or because it was the first rhyme?
